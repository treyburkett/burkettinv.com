What We Look For in a Deal

Every acquisition has to answer the same questions. Here's the framework we bring to each one, and why we pass on far more than we pursue.

Burkett Investments  ·  June 16, 2026  ·  5 min read

Good real estate outcomes aren't lucky, they're disciplined. The deals that work tend to share a handful of traits, and the ones that don't usually failed a test we could have run before we ever wrote an offer. Here is the framework we apply to every opportunity that crosses our desk.

It starts with the business plan, not the price

A price only means something next to a plan. Before we talk numbers, we get specific about what the asset is supposed to become: who lives or works there, what we'd change, what we wouldn't, and what the property looks like the day we sell it. If we can't articulate the plan in a few sentences, we're not ready to underwrite it, and we're certainly not ready to bid.

Durable demand

We want assets in places where demand shows up whether or not the cycle cooperates. That means looking past the headline market to the submarket and the block: the trajectory of jobs and population, the supply pipeline that competes with us, and the everyday reasons a resident or tenant chooses this location over the next one.

We underwrite the downside first. If the plan only works in the best case, it isn't a plan, it's a hope.

Basis and downside

Buying right is most of the job. We anchor on basis, what we're paying per unit or per foot relative to replacement cost and to recent, comparable trades, and we stress-test from there. Rates move, exits compress, lease-up takes longer than the broker's model says. We want a margin of safety that survives those assumptions being wrong, not just a return that looks good when everything goes right.

Operational upside we can actually execute

Value-add is only real if someone can run it. We favor NOI levers we know how to pull: operational discipline, smart capital projects, and a resident or tenant experience that supports rent and retention. The question isn't whether upside exists on paper; it's whether our team can deliver it on the ground.

Capital that matches the plan

The right capital structure follows the hold, not the other way around. We size debt and structure equity to the business plan's timeline and risk, so that a longer lease-up or a softer exit window is an inconvenience, not an existential threat. Leverage should amplify a sound plan, never prop up a shaky one.

Alignment

Finally, we look in the mirror. We bring an institutional process and a client-first philosophy to every engagement, and we're at the table on the decisions that matter. If our interests and our partners' interests aren't pointed the same direction, no spreadsheet fixes that.

The throughline

Understand, underwrite, execute, steward. It's the same discipline at every stage, and it's why we say no a lot more than we say yes. If you're weighing an acquisition or thinking through a hold, we'd welcome the conversation.
