What we look for in a deal

Burkett Investments6 min read

Most deals die in the first hour, and that is by design. We look at a lot of opportunities and pass on nearly all of them. The point of underwriting is not to find a reason to buy. It is to find every reason not to, and then see what survives.

Here is the short version of what a deal has to clear before we recommend committing capital.

1. Demand that does not depend on a story

We start with the market, not the building. Is there real, durable demand here, driven by jobs, migration, and household formation, or is the thesis a spreadsheet extrapolation of the last three good years? We want submarkets where people are already choosing to live and work, not ones that require a narrative to justify. If the demand only exists in the pro forma, the deal is already over.

2. Basis before upside

You make your money when you buy. We underwrite to a basis that works at today's rents and today's rates, not to an exit cap that assumes the market keeps cooperating. If a deal only clears because of aggressive rent growth or a compressed exit, it is not a deal, it is a bet on conditions we do not control. A defensible basis is what lets you hold through a bad year instead of being forced to sell into one.

3. Cash flow that survives the downside

We stress the downside first. What happens to coverage if rents flatten, expenses run hot, and the refinance window closes? A deal that only works in the base case is fragile. The assets we like throw off cash that holds up when the plan does not go perfectly, because the plan never goes perfectly. Durable cash flow is boring, and boring is the point.

4. An operational thesis, not just a spread

Because we advise operators and owners through the hold, we have to be able to say exactly how an asset gets better: the units, the expenses, the resident experience, the vendor contracts, the insurance program. If we cannot draw a straight line from acquisition to a specific operational improvement, the return is just financial engineering, and financial engineering does not survive contact with a soft quarter. The best deals have a to-do list, not just a spread.

5. Alignment and a real hold

We recommend capital where clients are willing to stay. That means alignment on horizon, on risk, and on what winning looks like. We are not interested in recommending something we would be embarrassed to hold for a decade, and we are not interested in partners who need an exit before the business plan is even in motion. The long hold is where connected advice actually pays off.

What kills a deal on the first read

A basis that only works with heroic assumptions. A market propped up by a single employer or a single trend. Deferred maintenance the seller is hoping you will not price. A capital stack with no room for a bad year. And a story that gets longer every time you ask a simple question. When the explanation grows, the conviction shrinks.

None of this is exotic. It is discipline applied consistently, which is rarer than it sounds. If you have a deal you think can clear these filters, we would like to see it.
